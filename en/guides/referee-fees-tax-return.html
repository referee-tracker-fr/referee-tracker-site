Referee match fees and your tax return

Every spring the same question comes back: do you have to declare your referee fees? In most cases, no, thanks to an exemption threshold. Here is how to know where you stand.

The rule: an exemption threshold

In France, money received by a referee or sports official who is not employed by their federation benefits from an allowance. As long as the total over a calendar year stays under 14.5% of the annual social security ceiling, it is exempt from:

  • social contributions, CSG and CRDS (article L. 241-16 of the Social Security Code);
  • income tax (article 93 of the General Tax Code).

The threshold counts all organisers together: county, region, tournaments, events. What matters is the yearly total, not the number of games.

The social security ceiling changes every year: check the amount on the Urssaf website for the year you need.

Careful: calendar year, not season

Your season runs from September to June, but the threshold is counted from 1 January to 31 December. A busy season can therefore spread over two years. To get your total, add up everything you received during the calendar year, whatever season the games belong to.

Another trap: a fee is often paid several weeks after the game. The date you received the money is what counts for the year.

What if you go over the threshold?

Above it, the money is no longer fully exempt. For the Urssaf, only the part above the threshold is normally subject to contributions. For income tax, specific rules apply. In that case, check impots.gouv.fr or ask your tax office before filling in your return.

Records to keep

  • Payment statements from each organiser.
  • Your bank statements showing the transfers.
  • Your appointments, to find each game's date and venue.
  • A yearly summary: date, organiser, amount received.

Keep them for several years: the tax office may ask for them.

Please note: this guide gives general explanations for France. It is not tax advice: for your own situation, check impots.gouv.fr, the Urssaf or your tax office.

Basketball referee: 7 habits to improve

Improving as a referee isn't just about the number of games. Small habits, kept up all season, make the biggest difference.

1. Review your games on video

This is the habit that makes the biggest difference. On court you see a play from one angle, in a split second. On video you can watch it again, slow it down, check your positioning and your partner's. No need to watch everything: pick five to ten plays per game, the ones where you hesitated or that got a reaction. Our guide Video analysis for referees explains how.

2. Revise the rules a little, often

Ten questions a day beat a whole evening before an exam. Regular revision makes the rules stick, especially the special cases: the ones that come up on a Saturday night with two minutes to go. Mix the topics: violations, fouls, clocks, throw-in situations.

3. Debrief every game

Right after the game, take two minutes with your partner. One simple question: "what would we do differently?" Write the answer down. After a few weeks the same topics keep coming back: those are your real development points.

4. Keep track of your goals

After an assessment, your referee coach gives you points to work on: positioning on drives, bench management, communication. Write them down and read them before each game. A goal you don't re-read is forgotten within two weekends.

5. Stay fit

A tired referee arrives late on the play, and a late referee makes more mistakes. Two sessions a week are enough to keep the pace: one endurance session and one short interval session, which matches the efforts of a game. Add some ankle and knee strengthening.

6. Watch others referee

Watching a higher-level game while focusing on the referees, not the play, teaches a lot: their positioning, their signals, how they talk to players and coaches. Take notes on what you want to borrow.

7. Track your season in numbers

Number of games, levels refereed, assessments received: seeing your progress over a season is motivating and helps plan the next one. Our guide Planning your season gives a simple method.

Traps to avoid

  • Only reviewing your mistakes: reviewing what worked helps you do it again.
  • Trying to fix everything at once: one or two goals at a time, not ten.
  • Refereeing without warming up: injuries often happen early in the game.
  • Waiting for an assessment to question yourself: a debrief with your partner is available after every game.

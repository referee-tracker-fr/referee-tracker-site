The French mileage scale, explained simply

What a car really costs per kilometre, according to the French tax authority.

1What is the scale for?

It gives a flat cost per kilometre covering depreciation, maintenance, tyres, fuel and insurance. Tolls and parking are not included.

It is mainly used by people who declare their actual expenses. For a referee, it is also a benchmark of what driving to games really costs.

Source: scale published by the French tax authority for the 2026 return on 2025 income (impots.gouv.fr). It can change from one year to the next: check the one for the year you need.

3Finding your car's power

It is on your registration certificate, box P.6, in fiscal horsepower (CV). The scale stops at 7 CV: above that, use the "7 CV or more" row.

4Two examples

5 CV · 4,000 km€2,5444,000 × 0.636 · about €0.64/km
6 CV · 8,000 km€4,449(8,000 × 0.374) + 1,457 · about €0.56/km

Several cars? Work it out car by car, without adding the kilometres together.

5Tax scale vs travel allowance

The mileage allowance you are paid for a game is set by the organiser, with its own rate. It has nothing to do with the tax scale.

Comparing the two shows whether your trips cost you more than they bring in, especially when fuel is expensive.

Please note: general explanations for France, not tax advice. For your situation, check impots.gouv.fr or your tax office.

The French mileage scale, explained simply

The French mileage scale estimates what a car really costs per kilometre. For a referee who drives a lot, it is a useful benchmark. Here is how to read it, with examples.

What is the scale for?

Published every year by the French tax authority, the mileage scale gives a flat cost per kilometre based on your car's power and the distance driven in the year. It covers depreciation, maintenance, tyres, fuel and insurance. Tolls and parking are not included.

It is mainly used by people who declare their actual expenses. For a referee, it is also a good benchmark of what driving to games really costs.

The 2026 table (2025 income)

d = kilometres driven in the year with that car.

3 CV or lessd × 0.529(d × 0.316) + 1,065d × 0.370
4 CVd × 0.606(d × 0.340) + 1,330d × 0.407
5 CVd × 0.636(d × 0.357) + 1,395d × 0.427
6 CVd × 0.665(d × 0.374) + 1,457d × 0.447
7 CV or mored × 0.697(d × 0.394) + 1,515d × 0.470

For a fully electric car, the result is increased by 20%. Source: scale published by the French tax authority for the 2026 return on 2025 income (impots.gouv.fr). The scale can change from one year to the next: always check the one for the year you need.

Finding your car's power

The fiscal horsepower (CV) is on your registration certificate, box P.6. The scale stops at 7 CV: a more powerful car uses the "7 CV or more" row.

Two examples

  • 5 CV, 4,000 km in the year: 4,000 × 0.636 = €2,544, about €0.64 per kilometre.
  • 6 CV, 8,000 km in the year: (8,000 × 0.374) + 1,457 = €4,449, about €0.56 per kilometre.

If you use several cars, work it out car by car, without adding the kilometres together.

Tax scale and travel allowance are two different things

The mileage allowance you are paid for a game is set by the organiser, with its own rate. It has nothing to do with the tax scale. Comparing the two shows whether your trips cost you more than they bring in, especially when fuel is expensive.

Please note: this guide gives general explanations for France. It is not tax advice: for your own situation, check impots.gouv.fr, the Urssaf or your tax office.

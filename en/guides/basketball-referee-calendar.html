Basketball referee calendar: never miss a game

Between games, time changes, cancellations and personal life, a referee's calendar fills up fast. Here's how to keep it up to date without effort.

Why the calendar is a problem

A referee receives games through a series of messages: an appointment, then sometimes a time change, a new venue, a cancellation, a postponement. If every message has to be copied into a calendar by hand, a single slip is enough to turn up at the wrong time, or to block a Saturday that has become free.

1. One source for all your games

First principle: one single place holding all your games, up to date. Not a mix of messages, screenshots and notes. If you receive appointments by e-mail, group them in a dedicated folder with a rule in your mailbox. Everything else builds on that.

2. Update on every change

When a message announces a change, update the calendar straight away, not in the evening. Delete cancelled games: a ghost game in the calendar also skews your end-of-month accounts. For postponements, create the new slot as soon as the date is known.

3. Add your departure time, not just tip-off

The time that matters is when you have to leave home. Add the venue address and travel time to the event, then a reminder set to your departure time. For an 8 pm game an hour's drive away, with an early arrival expected, your real appointment is around 6 pm.

4. Manage your availability

Block the weekends you're unavailable well in advance: exams, holidays, family events. Telling your appointments officer early avoids having to turn games down at the last minute. Keep an eye on your workload too: several games in one weekend is possible, but only with compatible travel.

5. Share the calendar with family

A calendar shared with your family avoids surprises: everyone can see when you're refereeing and when you'll be back. Most calendars (Google, Apple, Outlook) can show a subscribed calendar next to your personal one.

6. Useful reminders, not too many

  • The evening before: check the appointment and pack your bag (see our pre-game checklist).
  • At departure time: leave.
  • After the game: mark it as refereed to track its payment.

Classic mistakes

  • Relying on the first version of an appointment that has since changed.
  • Writing down tip-off without the expected arrival time.
  • Keeping a cancelled game in the calendar, then counting it in your fees.
  • Forgetting to declare unavailability, and having to turn down a game at the last minute.

What an app changes

With Referee Tracker, you forward your appointments with a rule in your mailbox: each game is created automatically, then updated if it changes or is cancelled. Your games appear in Apple Calendar, Google Calendar or Outlook, updated automatically, and reminders alert you the day before and when it's time to leave. You don't copy anything any more.
